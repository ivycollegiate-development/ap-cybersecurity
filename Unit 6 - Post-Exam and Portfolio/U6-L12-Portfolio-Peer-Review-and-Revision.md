## PART 1 — THE REVIEW PROTOCOL (12 min)

**Read your partner's portfolio the way a stranger would, and in the same order a stranger would.** The order is not arbitrary; the first thirty seconds decides whether anything else gets read.

**Protocol, timed, 10 minutes per pair. Two of you, swapping halfway:**

1. **The thirty-second read (2 min).** Open the README only. Read for thirty seconds, then close it and write, without looking:
   - ☐ What kind of security person does this person want to be?
   - ☐ What is the single strongest thing here, and why?
   - ☐ Is there anything in here I do not understand?

   **The third question is the most valuable one and it is the one nobody asks out loud.** Every unclear sentence you found is a sentence your portfolio is losing readers on — probably in an interview, where you cannot ask. **A portfolio that requires the author to be present is not a portfolio.**

2. **The one-click test (1 min).** From the README, reach the strongest piece in one click. If it takes two, that is a finding.

3. **The best piece, properly (5 min).** Read the featured piece straight through as a technical reader. Answer:
   - ☐ Can I follow the reasoning, or am I being shown conclusions?
   - ☐ Does every claim have evidence, or are some asserted?
   - ☐ Is the "what I would do differently" section real, or is it a humblebrag? **The test: does it name a specific thing that is actually wrong or missing, or does it only praise the author?**
   - ☐ If I had to ask the author one question, what would it be? **Write that question down and give it to them.** This is the single most useful thing you will produce today.

4. **The talk (2 min).** Listen to their three-minute talk. Do not be kind.
   - ☐ Did the hook make me care?
   - ☐ Did I understand the scope without being told?
   - ☐ Was there a decision explained, or a list of things described?
   - ☐ Did the honest section sound honest, or did it sound like an apology?
   - ☐ Where did my attention go? **That timestamp is the note that matters most** — the moment you started looking at your phone is the moment their structure failed.

## PART 2 — THE RUBRIC, AND HOW I WILL USE IT (6 min)

**We score each other on six criteria, 1–4 each. Score honestly — a kind score today is a wasted day for you, because you will not fix what nobody mentioned.**

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|-----------|---|---|---|---|
| **Clarity to a stranger** | I could not follow it | Followed with effort | Followed easily | Followed effortlessly; no author needed |
| **Evidence** | Claims asserted | Some evidence | Most claims evidenced | Every claim evidenced or explicitly marked as inference |
| **Reasoning shown** | Conclusions only | Thin reasoning | Reasoning present | Reasoning connects every decision to a constraint |
| **Honest limits** | Absent or performative | Vague | Specific | Specific and genuinely self-critical |
| **Navigable in 30 sec** | Lost immediately | Found the pieces eventually | Navigable | Immediate; strong piece obvious |
| **Talk delivery** | Rambling or apologetic | Read the notes | Conversational | Structured, and the honest part landed |

**How this becomes your grade, and I want to be transparent about it:** the review and revision count as the deliverable. **A portfolio that gets a low score and is then substantially revised scores better than a strong portfolio that ignored its feedback** — because the thing being assessed today is your ability to receive specific criticism and act on it, which is the professional skill and the one you will need most in your first job.

## PART 3 — REVISION TIME (20 min)

**This is the work. The review is only worth what you do with it.**

**Take the feedback and sort it into three piles, on paper:**

| **FIX** — specific, actionable, cheap | **CONSIDER** — real but expensive or arguable | **DEFER** — not now, and here is why |
|-----------------------------------|--------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| | | |

**Then fix the FIX pile, in this priority order:**

1. **☐ Every unclear sentence your reviewer found.** This first, and always first, because clarity is the multiplier on everything else. **A portfolio nobody fully understands cannot be rescued by good content.**
2. **☐ Any claim without evidence, or asserted rather than shown.** Either supply the evidence, or **cut the claim, or mark it explicitly as an expectation rather than a finding.** All three are acceptable. Asserting it as established is not.
3. **☐ The one-click path to your best piece.** If it takes two clicks, fix the links. It takes five minutes and it is the difference between a reviewer finding your work and not.
4. **☐ The talk, at the exact point your reviewer checked out.** **That timestamp is your instruction.** Rework that one segment, then time it again. Do not rehearse the whole talk — fix the break.
5. **☐ The through-line sentence**, if your reviewer's answer to "what kind of security person is this?" was vague or wrong. **If they got it wrong, it is not that they misread you; it is that the sentence is not doing its job.** Rewrite it and put it higher up.
6. **☐ Broken links, and anything you claimed you would do and did not.**

**Disagreeing with feedback is allowed and sometimes correct.** If you reject a piece of feedback, **write down that you rejected it and why, in one line.** The ability to evaluate criticism rather than obey it is itself a professional judgement, and having the reasoning written down is what distinguishes the two.

**Then, and this is the last substantive task of the course:** **cut 15%.** Find the paragraph that restates something already said, the sentence that exists to sound thorough, and the hedge that says nothing. **Cut them.** A portfolio improved by cutting is a portfolio that will be finished when you present it.

## PART 4 — TOMORROW'S PRESENTATION, FINAL CHECK (7 min)

**Five minutes, and here is the frame one last time:**

- ☐ **0:00–0:30** — Who I am, what I did this year, the strongest piece. The README spoken.
- ☐ **0:30–3:30** — **The three-minute talk.** The centre of the presentation. Your best piece, your reasoning, your honest part.
- ☐ **3:30–4:30** — Two other pieces, one line each. **Point at them; do not summarise them.**
- ☐ **4:30–5:00** — The through-line and what is next. **Then stop. Do not trail off.**
- ☐ **Q&A follows.** The best question you can prepare for is the one your reviewer wrote down for you. **Have that answer ready, and if you do not know it, say so and say what you would look up.** In a technical room, *"I don't know, and here is how I would find out"* is a strong answer. A confident guess is the only thing that reliably loses you the room.

**Final practical checks, tonight:**
- ☐ Portfolio open and reachable. **If it is on GitHub, confirm it is public.** A portfolio nobody can open scores zero and this is the one avoidable zero available to you.
- ☐ Links tested one final time, on a device that is not the one you built it on if you can manage it.
- ☐ Opening and closing memorised. Middle can be conversational.
- ☐ **Know your first thirty seconds by heart.** Nerves hit first, and the opening is what you will lose if you let them.

## 🇹🇼 TAIWAN CONTEXT

**3-minute brief, and this is the last one of the year.**

**Give feedback on the local context, and do it specifically, because this is where a portfolio most often fails with a foreign reader.** In your review, answer one of these two:

- ☐ **The reader test:** *I am an international reader with no background in this. In your best piece, tell me one place where you used a local fact and I could not follow why it mattered.* **That is a precise, actionable piece of feedback**, and it is the most useful thing you can give someone in this room.
- ☐ **The strength test:** *Does the local context make the work sound more credible, or does it sound like an explanation of why the work is interesting?* **Those are different and only one of them helps.** A portfolio that keeps apologising for being local, or keeps insisting on its local importance, reads as insecure. A portfolio where the local work is simply presented as the work, on equal terms with everything else, reads as confident.

**The right shape, stated once more for tomorrow:** define the term in a clause, then speak your reader's language for the rest of it. Confidence plus context plus specifics. Nothing overstated, nothing apologised for.

**And the closing thought for the block, said plainly because it is true:** the local threat knowledge is a genuine professional advantage, and it is only ever an advantage when paired with fundamentals and delivered without defensiveness. You have the fundamentals — a full year of CED content and an AP exam behind it. What you do in five minutes tomorrow is present them like someone who has them.
