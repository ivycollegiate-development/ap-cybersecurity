# CED Excerpt — Unit 1 Topics Overview

*Source: AP Cybersecurity Course and Exam Description (College Board, 2026)*

## Topic 1.1 — Understanding Social Engineering

**Essential Knowledge:**
- Social engineering exploits human psychology (urgency, authority, familiarity, trust)
- Attackers use pretexting, phishing, vishing, and impersonation to bypass technical controls
- The S.E.T. framework (Social Engineering Tactics) categorizes psychological triggers
- Multi-factor authentication and verification protocols reduce social engineering risk

## Topic 1.2 — Suspicious Website Logins

**Essential Knowledge:**
- Login logs contain: timestamp, device, IP address, success/failure status
- Suspicious patterns: unfamiliar IPs, off-hours access, repeated failures, multiple geographies
- Attackers use credential stuffing (breached credentials reused across sites), brute force (trying all combinations), and password spraying (common passwords across many accounts)
- MFA fatigue occurs when attackers bombard a user with push notifications until they approve

## Topic 1.3 — Best Practices for Public Networks

**Essential Knowledge:**
- Public Wi-Fi is shared medium — traffic can be intercepted
- Evil twin attacks impersonate legitimate networks to capture credentials
- Deauth attacks disconnect devices from a legitimate network, forcing reconnection to a rogue AP
- VPNs, HTTPS, and disabling auto-connect are primary protections
- Certificate validation prevents man-in-the-middle attacks

## Topic 1.4 — AI-Based Cybersecurity Attacks

**Essential Knowledge:**
- Generative AI creates convincing phishing content with no grammar errors
- AI voice cloning and deepfake video enable impersonation attacks
- AI automates vulnerability scanning, exploitation, and adaptive malware
- AI agents can operate autonomously — escaping sandboxes, coordinating, and covering tracks
- Attack amplification: AI not only executes attacks but designs new attack tools

## Topic 1.5 — Leveraging AI in Cyber Defense

**Essential Knowledge:**
- AI assists defenders through anomaly detection at scale
- Automated code review (CodeQL, AI assistants) finds vulnerabilities faster than manual review
- SIEM systems use AI to triage alerts and prioritize threats
- AI incident response tools suggest remediation steps
- Effective defense requires: visibility, audit trails, human oversight, and review of AI-generated actions
