## Teacher Notes & Instructions

**Setup checklist:**
- ☑ Print one copy per student — paper day, no computers needed
- Deck: 1.2_Suspicious_Website_Logins (25 slides) projected while students work
- Whole deck review — every section maps to slides (numbers in the headers)
- Section 4 ranking: pairs argue the order, defend especially P@ssw0rd
- Section 8: choose ONE prompt; Option A pairs with the deck's Turn & Talk slide

**Answer key (quick reference):**
- §1: Guessed / Stolen / Reused / Breached; real users vs. attackers grinding guesses — job is telling them apart
- §2: burst of failed logins; logins at times you'd never log in; unknown devices. Table: 1, 2, 3. Quick check: B
- §3: examples vary (formula = word + year + !); "thousands" / "in the list"; Maya's facts: birthday Mar 15 2007, volleyball #7, cat Luna, Lincoln High class of 2025; dictionary attack / ONE
- §4: weakest→strongest: P@ssw0rd ≈ Buddy2025! (both formula/known), then Buddy2025!, then kX9mPq#2vL$8wR, strongest purple-elephant-tuesday-galaxy (long random words beat symbol-noise of dictionary length; kX9mPq is strong but hard to remember, passphrase is long AND memorable). P@ssw0rd weak: leetspeak of "password" is in every cracking dictionary
- §5: Long / Random / Unique; password manager = generates + stores long random unique passwords, you memorize ONE master password; passphrase = string of random unrelated words, long but memorable. Quick check 1: B. Quick check 2: C
- §6: texted code, authenticator app, biometric, security key; the password alone isn't enough — no phone/fingerprint/key means they're stuck. Quick check: C
- §7: order = 2, 4, 1, 3 (breached → tried everywhere → email master key → total takeover). Quick check: C. Rewind: password manager (unique 16-char per account) + MFA on email
- §8: grade for thinking — rubric: does the student connect a real experience to the warning signs (A), reason about why email is the master key with concrete attacker steps (B), or explain meter-vs-reality with the formula/dictionary logic (C)
