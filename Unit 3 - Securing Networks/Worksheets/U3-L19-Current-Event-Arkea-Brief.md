## Part A: The Incident (read — 3 min)

On 4–5 November 2026, Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, a French regional banking group, confirmed it had suffered a cyberattack in which attackers made **fraudulent transfers from the bank's own accounts** to accounts held at other banks. The bank states the incident is contained, that customer accounts were not affected, and that no client lost money. It describes the attack as sophisticated and external, and says it was **not** the result of employee error. It has not disclosed how the attackers gained access.

The loss figure is **not confirmed by the bank**. Reports cite roughly €100–118 million ($112–133 million). The figure was first published by ZachXBT, an independent blockchain investigator, who posted an alert on Telegram **two days before** the bank publicly acknowledged anything. In other words, an outside researcher surfaced the theft before the bank's own monitoring did.

The stolen funds were converted to cryptocurrency and dispersed across accounts on multiple platforms. Two statements about the aftermath conflict. A France 24 security expert said recovery is "virtually impossible." The bank said "a portion" of the funds was blocked and that recovery efforts continue. Both cannot be fully accurate. The bank has also not confirmed the total amount taken. For scale: the group reported roughly €230 million in net profit for the first half of the year, so a €100 million-plus loss is on the order of half a half-year's earnings.
