# U3 L22: Flex / Catch-Up Day (Jan 4, Mon — TECH DAY)

**First day back.** Sixteen days off — the longest gap of the year. Expect a warm-up period; use it deliberately rather than pretending the break did not happen.

**Learning Objectives:**
- 4.1.A Identify attack surface on a personally owned device and name the vulnerability class it creates
- 4.2.C Compare authentication factors and explain what each one contributes to a login decision
- 4.4.C State what a host-based detection tool observes that a network-based tool cannot

**Materials:** Printed return-day tracker (running list of outstanding submissions), device audit packet for collection, the two Unit 4 decks from Dec 16–17 (Stuxnet case study, 4.1 types of malware) queued for re-entry, laptops for resubmission

---

## Activities

1. **Attendance and check-in (4 min):** Confirm who is back. Write your name against the item on the printed tracker. If you have no idea what you owe, say so — that is a conversation, not a penalty. Collect the device audits **on paper, at the door**, before anyone sits down.

2. **Graded HW quiz on the break audit (18 min):** Paper quiz. 5 questions built directly from the audit packet: one on identifying attack surface versus device inventory, one on a malware type and the control that blocks it, one on authentication (reuse, MFA, which factor is doing the work), one on hardening and residual risk, one on a Taiwan addendum. **Answers are the student's own audit, read back.** This is why the audit was hand-written and brought in — a photo on a phone does not get graded today.

   Then go over it while it is still in their hands. Mark the packet against the four-line standard — **describe, assess, treat, evidence** — and point at the specific line that earned or lost each point. Most lost points are a missing evidence line or a treatment with no reasoning sentence.

3. **Silent catch-up work (14 min):** Complete any outstanding submission from the tracker, or use the time to start the Unit 4 study guide. Work independently; raise your hand if you are stuck on a specific item and I will come to you.

4. **Re-entry — the two lessons the break sat in the middle of (10 min):** The Dec 15–17 Unit 4 lessons — **4.1 What Counts as a Computing Device**, the **Stuxnet case study**, and **4.1 Types of Malware** — were taught three weeks ago. Do not re-teach them. Walk the room in five minutes: one prompt per deck, students answer from memory, cold. Stuxnet: what made it different from every piece of malware before it? Malware: name the type, name the control. Use the misses to decide what Unit 4 needs to re-cover on Tuesday, and note the two or three on the tracker for a whole-class re-entry rather than re-teaching it to everyone.

5. **Close-out (4 min):** Anything still open goes on the tracker and gets first claim tomorrow. Remind the room that the **Unit 4 test is Friday, Jan 29** — so 4.2 authentication, 4.3 hardening, and 4.4 detection all have to be picked up in the two weeks after midterms, not the week before.

**Midterms are Jan 18–21 and there is no class those days.** Classes resume Friday, Jan 22 with the Host IDS lab, then Unit 4 continues through 4.2 authentication (4.2.A–4.2.D), 4.3 hardening and patching and encryption, and 4.4 antivirus, EDR, and HIDS, with the test on Jan 29. The Saturday CTF sessions (Jan 16 and Jan 23) sit inside that stretch. There is no second break between here and then.

**Homework (due Mon Jan 11, 23:59):** Read CED 4.2–4.3 and bring two written examples: one authentication scheme you consider strong and one you consider weak, each with a sentence on what changes if the attacker has the password anyway. Finish any outstanding tracker item.

**Differentiation / ELL support:** The tracker is a checklist, so students can see exactly what is outstanding without having to track it themselves. The quiz is answerable in prose rather than MCQ, and every question can be answered with two or three sentences — say so, so nobody spirals about a 5-question paper. Students who lost the packet can reproduce their own findings from a blank template during the silent catch-up block and submit it by the end of the day, though it will be scored without the evidence lines. The re-entry prompts are recall, not assessment — do not grade them.
