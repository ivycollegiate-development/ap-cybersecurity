# Cybersecurity Credential Ladder — From This Class to a Career

*A companion guide for ICA AP Cybersecurity students and families*

---

## The honest starting point

You may have read that AI is automating entry-level tech jobs. That's partly true — for jobs whose main task is producing routine output: basic code, first drafts, standard reports. Cybersecurity is different in one specific way: **the core of the job is being the person accountable for a risk decision**. AI can scan logs all night; it cannot sign its name saying "this system is safe to ship." Every security team needs people who can make that call and defend it.

That's why cybersecurity demand keeps growing (the global workforce gap is roughly 4 million unfilled jobs) even as general software hiring tightens. But there are no shortcuts: certifications open doors, **judgment is what gets you hired**. This ladder shows how the two stack.

---

## Rung 1 — While you're at ICA (this course)

| What | Why it matters |
|------|----------------|
| **AP Cybersecurity course + exam** | College credit potential plus a real portfolio: GitHub repos with your fix history, attestation comments, lab writeups |
| **Free CompTIA Security+ voucher** (College Board AP Kickstart, score 3+) | A professional certification before university — most people pay ~$400 and study alone for months to get this |
| **Home lab / CTF writeups** | TryHackMe, PicoCTF, HackTheBox Academy free tiers. Write up what you did — these artifacts ARE your experience |

## Rung 2 — First credentials after graduation

- **CompTIA Security+ (already earned via Kickstart)** — qualifies you for many government/contractor roles and is the baseline HR filter for security jobs
- Optional add-ons depending on direction: **Network+** (networking depth), or skip straight to hands-on platforms
- University path note: a CS or IT degree still helps, but pair it with security work from day one — clubs, CTF teams, SOC internships. Don't wait for the degree to grant experience; manufacture it.

## Rung 3 — Breaking in (years 0–2)

The industry's open secret: "entry-level security" jobs mostly hire from IT support. The realistic first job is help desk / desktop support / junior sysadmin **at a company with a security team**, then move internally within 12–18 months. Choose employers for the ladder they contain, not the job title.

What gets you promoted out of support fast: showing your Security+, your CTF writeups, and asking the SOC team for shadowing.

## Rung 4 — Specialization (years 2–5)

Pick one lane and go deep:

| Lane | Credential | What it is |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Defensive operations (SOC) | **CySA+** → later **GCFA/GCIH** | Threat detection and incident response |
| Offensive (pentesting) | **PenTest+** → **OSCP** | Attacking systems legally; portfolio-heavy field |
| Governance / compliance / audit | **Security+ → CISA** or **CISM** track | Risk decisions, attestations, regulation — highly AI-resistant |
| Cloud security | **AWS/Azure security certs** | Every company's cloud needs hardening |

## Rung 5 — Senior standing

**CISSP** (requires 5 years experience), then leadership tracks (CISO, principal consultant). These are the mid-career credentials that this whole ladder builds toward — the ones where your judgment carries real liability and real compensation.

---

## Three rules that make the ladder work

1. **Every cert needs evidence next to it.** A cert says "I passed a test." A writeup says "here is me thinking." Employers increasingly screen for the second.
2. **AI tools are your assistants now — practice using them honestly.** In this class you use AI to find bugs and then attest to your own verification. Do the same professionally: let AI do the scanning, keep your name on the decision.
3. **Taiwan is the best place on earth to learn this.** It faces more cyberattacks per year than almost any country. Local threat knowledge — Chinese-language phishing, semiconductor-sector targeting — is expertise no generic overseas program replicates.
