## Question Answers

### 1. Where is the incident window boundary (first and last evidence record)?

- **First evidence record:** 01:52 — B-1104 entry at LOADING-DOCK (the first record inside the window; it is evidence that nothing happened, which is why it stays in the timeline even though it means nothing).
- **Last evidence record:** 04:10 — guard's "front entrance quiet" observation, which closes out the 04:05 MAIN-EAST entry.
- The guard's 01:30 "quiet, no visitors" is the window's opening anchor; everything after 04:10 (05:30 perimeter, 06:30 dock exit, morning arrivals) is context, not evidence of the incident.

### 2. Which person appears in one source but not the others?

**The person in the dark jacket at 02:14 (CAM-CORR-B).** They appear only in the CCTV descriptions — no badge is visible, they are absent from the badge log (unless they triggered B-2207), absent from the visitor log, and absent from the guard's report. Conversely, every person in the logs has corroboration: the cleaning crew (badge + camera), R. Chen (visitor log), H. Okafor (visitor log), M. Ortiz (patrol + camera), and the 04:05 facilities-uniform person (badge + camera, self-identified). The dark-jacketed person is the only human in the evidence who exists in exactly one source.

### 3. How do you reconcile the 02:14 SERVER-RM entry with the 04:05 MAIN-EAST entry for the same badge?

The badge log records a **credential**, not a person. Two entries for B-2207 nearly two hours apart and in different buildings force a choice, and the evidence supports a specific reading:

- **The legitimate reading (most consistent):** The real B-2207 holder entered SERVER-RM at 02:14, exited at 02:15 (a one-minute visit consistent with a maintenance check), then returned at 04:05 through MAIN-EAST and self-identified on camera. The door failed to re-latch after the 02:15 exit and was found ajar at 03:40.
- **The alternative reading:** Someone cloned or borrowed B-2207 and used it at 02:14, while the real holder arrived at 04:05. Supporting this: the 02:14 camera shows a person with **no visible badge**. Against it: the same camera cannot see the reader, and the 02:15 *exit* event shows a working credential both ways — a tailgater walking through an already-open door would not generate a clean entry/exit pair.
- The honest conclusion: the 02:14–02:15 entry/exit pair is **probably** the badge holder's own visit, but it is **not confirmed** — that is exactly why the finding must be labeled probable, not confirmed.

### 4. What time interval is covered by NO source at all?

**02:22 – 03:40 — 78 minutes.** The camera looped/recycled at 02:22 and recorded nothing until the guard appeared at 03:40. The badge log has no events between 02:15 and 04:05, and the guard was not in Corridor B between 01:30 and 03:40. This is the critical gap: the server room door was open (ajar by 03:40) for at least part of an interval in which **no source can say who was inside**. If anything happened in the server room, it happened here.

### 5. List every physical mechanism from Unit 2 that could explain the unexplained entry.

The evidence narrows but does not eliminate. Surviving mechanisms:

1. **Cloned badge** — a duplicate of B-2207 presented at 02:14 (fits the "same badge, two locations" tension).
2. **Tailgating through a door with a faulty latch** — the door was found ajar at 03:40; if it failed to latch after the 02:15 exit, an unbadged person (the dark jacket) could have entered behind the credential event or through the unlatched door (fits "no visible badge" on camera).
3. **Propped service door** — a service door could have been propped open during the 02:22–03:40 gap.
4. **Unlogged contractor with a temporary badge never deactivated** — a temporary credential issued to an earlier visitor (e.g., leftover from R. Chen's maintenance visit) could still be active.
5. **Someone already inside who never left** — a person remaining in the building after the 23:00 all-clear, with no badge event at all.

A group that commits to a single tidy explanation has over-read the evidence. The correct product is two or three surviving mechanisms plus the confidence labels.

## Model Incident Report (Sections 1, 3, 4 — for the differentiation example)

**Section 1 — Incident summary:** Between 02:14 and 03:40 on the night of the incident, the server room was accessed using badge B-2207 and subsequently found unsecured. No source covers the interval 02:22–03:40, so the identity of anyone inside the room during that period cannot be established.

**Section 3 — Findings:**
- Finding A — *Unconfirmed:* Badge B-2207 was used at SERVER-RM at 02:14 by the dark-jacketed person seen on CAM-CORR-B; the holder's own account of an early arrival at 04:05 is plausible but unverified.
- Finding B — *Confirmed:* The server room door was left unsecured for an interval ending at 03:40 (guard found it ajar; baseline at 00:30 was latched).
- Finding C — *Confirmed:* An unbadged person was present in Corridor B at 02:14 and unaccounted for in every other source.

**Section 4 — Gaps:** No camera covers the stairwell landing or the server room door itself, so the 02:14 badge event cannot be visually attributed; the 02:22–03:40 camera gap leaves the critical hour unrecorded; no badge-to-person roster was provided, so B-2207's assigned holder cannot be confirmed from the evidence alone.
