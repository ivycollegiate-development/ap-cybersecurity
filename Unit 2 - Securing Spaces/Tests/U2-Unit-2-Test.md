## Section I — Multiple Choice (20 points)

**1.** A contractor spends two mornings photographing badge readers, counting staff arrivals, and noting the hours when the loading dock is unattended. Which attack phase does this describe?

A. Preparatory / reconnaissance
B. Establishing access
C. Lateral movement
D. Exfiltration

**2.** An intruder uses a duplicated badge to open the back-of-house door of a building for the first time and steps inside. Which attack phase does this describe?

A. Preparatory / reconnaissance
B. Establishing access
C. Lateral movement
D. Exfiltration

**3.** An intruder who is already inside the building walks an unescorted service corridor to the key cabinet, takes a key, and opens the records room. Which attack phase does this describe?

A. Preparatory / reconnaissance
B. Establishing access
C. Lateral movement
D. Exfiltration

**4.** A building's flood exposure has a single loss expectancy (SLE) of **NT$6,000,000** and an annualized loss expectancy (ALE) of **NT$1,500,000**. What is the annualized rate of occurrence (ARO)?

A. 0.25
B. 4.0
C. 0.75
D. 0.15

**5.** A finding is rated **High likelihood / High impact**. The switch gear and the records cabinet are then moved from the ground floor to the third floor, and the finding is re-rated **Medium likelihood / Medium impact**. Which statement is correct?

A. Medium/Medium is the inherent risk and High/High is the residual risk.
B. Medium/Medium is the residual risk, and the control reduced impact as well as likelihood.
C. Medium/Medium is the residual risk, and the control reduced likelihood only.
D. The residual risk is zero once the control is installed.

**6.** A control costs **NT$900,000 per year**. The ALE before the control is **NT$2,400,000**; the ALE with the control in place is **NT$600,000**. Which conclusion is best supported?

A. The control is not justified, because it does not remove the risk entirely.
B. The control is justified because the ALE with the control is below NT$900,000.
C. The control should be rejected because NT$900,000 is a large absolute cost.
D. The control is justified, because the annual reduction in expected loss (NT$1,800,000) exceeds its annual cost.

**7.** Which action is an example of **risk avoidance** rather than risk mitigation?

A. Installing a self-closing mechanism and a door-prop alarm on a fire exit.
B. Buying insurance that covers losses from forced entry.
C. Stopping the practice of storing scanned guest passports on site.
D. Documenting that the residual risk is acceptable.

**8.** Which action **transfers** risk?

A. Rekeying every lock on the ground floor.
B. Moving the network closet from the ground floor to the third floor.
C. Recording in the audit that a finding will be reviewed next year.
D. Purchasing a policy that covers losses caused by forced entry.

**9.** An intruder who is already inside the records room carries three backup drives out through the loading dock and leaves the site. Which attack phase does this describe?

A. Preparatory / reconnaissance
B. Establishing access
C. Exfiltration
D. Lateral movement

**10.** A propped fire door creates a repeated high-impact exposure. A door-prop alarm would reduce the annualized loss expectancy from **NT$1,200,000** to **NT$300,000** for a one-time cost of **NT$40,000**. Which treatment is **least** appropriate?

A. Mitigate
B. Avoid
C. Mitigate, then insure the residual
D. Accept

**11.** Four people enter the staff door on a single badge read, and the access log records one event. Which vulnerability does this demonstrate?

A. Shoulder surfing
B. Tailgating (piggybacking)
C. Credential cloning
D. Lock picking

**12.** A visitor standing at the reception counter watches a technician type the closet door PIN, then later enters that PIN. Which attack does this describe?

A. Shoulder surfing
B. Tailgating
C. Social engineering at the door
D. Credential cloning

**13.** A person in a high-visibility vest carrying a toolbox tells a staff member, "I'm here to check the fire panel," and the staff member releases the door without checking a work order. Which attack does this describe?

A. Tailgating
B. Shoulder surfing
C. Social engineering at the door (pretexting / impersonation)
D. Lock picking

**14.** A badge is read remotely by a concealed reader carried past a staff member's lanyard, and a working duplicate opens the same doors eleven days later. Which control most directly prevents the **duplicate** from working?

A. Printing a photograph of the holder on the badge
B. Replacing the PIN with a longer PIN
C. Issuing cards that authenticate cryptographically, so a copied identifier alone is not accepted
D. Reviewing the badge log at the end of each week

**15.** Which control most directly **prevents** tailgating, as opposed to detecting it afterwards?

A. A camera above the door, reviewed weekly
B. A mantrap or single-person interlock with anti-tailgating sensors
C. A visitor log at the reception desk
D. An end-of-day badge log review

**16.** A building has twelve CCTV cameras and still cannot show who entered the network closet. Which explanation is most consistent with 2.3.B?

A. Coverage is judged by whether the field of view captures the approach path and the door face, not by camera count.
B. Twelve cameras is below the recommended minimum for a building of that size.
C. Cameras cannot record at night without additional lighting.
D. Badge logs are a more reliable source than video in every case.

**17.** A ground-floor network closet in Taichung floods during typhoon season. Which control set **best** addresses the environmental risk?

A. Raising the equipment above the expected flood level, a water-leak sensor with an alert, and secondary drainage or a backflow valve.
B. A UPS and a second air-conditioning unit.
C. A camera aimed at the closet door and a visitor log for the floor.
D. A mantrap at the building entrance and rekeyed interior doors.

**18.** A CCTV exception report shows a line-crossing trigger on the loading-dock camera at 23:40, a time with no scheduled deliveries. What does that record prove?

A. It identifies the person who crossed the line.
B. It proves the loading dock was locked at the time.
C. It proves an intrusion occurred.

**19.** The badge log shows no read at the back-of-house door between 02:19 and 03:02, while the corridor camera records a person standing at the closet door at 02:19. Which pair of explanations is most plausible?

A. The camera clock is wrong, and the badge log was deleted.

C. Badge logs record door status, so the absence of a read proves the door stayed shut.

**20.** One site generates about 400 door events a night and has three staff on duty. Which alert design is best?

A. Alert on every door event, so that nothing is missed.
B. Alert on nothing, and review the log weekly.
C. Alert on every event between 18:00 and 06:00.
D. Alert on a denied read followed by a granted read at the same reader within two minutes, and on any read in a zone with no scheduled activity.

## Section II — Free Response (10 points)

**Time: 15 minutes. One question. Answer all five parts.**

### Scenario

A 42-room business hotel in Xitun District, Taichung, occupies six floors. On the ground floor: the lobby, the front desk, and a locked electrical-and-network closet beside the public restrooms. Housekeeping and maintenance staff badge through a back-of-house door. Security consists of **one camera above the front desk**, which sees the corridor entrance to the restrooms but **not** the closet door; a **badge log** for staff; **no visitor log**; and a **standard interior knob lock** on the closet — there is no card reader on the closet door. A door-contact sensor on the closet writes to the same access-control system but raises **no alarm**, and badge-log exceptions are **emailed to the facilities manager**, who reads them the next morning.

**Costs:** a restricted-keyway lockset and a closet alarm cost **NT$35,000**. The closet's equipment and one day of outage is valued at **NT$1,200,000**.

### Log extract — back-of-house door and closet sensor, 02:00–04:00

| Time | Credential / device | Event | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 02:07 | B-114 housekeeping (shift 07:00–15:00) | entry, back-of-house | granted |
| 02:11 | B-114 housekeeping | entry, back-of-house | denied — already inside |
| 02:12 | B-201 maintenance (shift 09:00–18:00) | entry, back-of-house | granted |
| 02:19 | closet door contact sensor | door open | no back-of-house badge event within 5 minutes |
| 03:02 | B-114 housekeeping | exit, back-of-house | granted |
| 03:40 | B-201 maintenance | exit, back-of-house | granted |

### CCTV exception report — front desk camera, same window

| Time | Trigger | Frame notes |
|---|---|---|
| 02:05–02:09 | motion, lobby | no person visible |
| 02:12 | line-crossing, front desk counter | one person, hood up, face not identifiable |
| 03:38 | motion, corridor to restrooms | two persons |

### Scoring rubric — 10 points (2 per part)

| Part | Points | Full-credit criteria |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | 2 | Identifies **two** physical vulnerabilities correctly and labels the CED category for each (**2.2.A, 2.2.B, 2.2.C**) — 1 point each |
| (b) | 2 | Names **one control per vulnerability (2.3.A / 2.3.B / 2.3.C)** and explains how it defeats **that specific mechanism**, not a control in general — 1 point each |
| (c) | 2 | Selects a treatment (**avoid / transfer / mitigate / accept**) for each finding and justifies it against the stated cost of the control and the size of the impact — 1 point each |
| (d) | 2 | States what the badge log **and the closet sensor record** prove and what they **cannot** prove (2.4.A), correlates them with the camera record, and gives **two** plausible explanations for the 02:19 event (2.4.B) — 1 point for the limitation, 1 point for the correlation plus both explanations |
| (e) | 2 | Writes an alerting threshold with a **named action, named owner, and time bound** (2.4.C), and explains why emailing the facilities manager fails the same objective — 1 point each |

### (a) Vulnerabilities

Identify two physical vulnerabilities in this scenario. For each, name the attack it enables and label it 2.2.A, 2.2.B, or 2.2.C.

### (b) Controls

For each vulnerability in (a), name one control (2.3.A, 2.3.B, or 2.3.C) and explain how it defeats that specific mechanism.

### (c) Treatment

For each finding, select a treatment — avoid, transfer, mitigate, or accept — and justify it against the cost of the control and the size of the impact.

### (d) The 02:19 event

What do the badge log and the closet sensor record prove about the closet door, and what can they not prove? Correlate them with the camera record and give **two** plausible explanations for the 02:19 door-open with no badge event.

### (e) Alerting

Write an alert threshold and its response for the closet door that satisfies 2.4.C. It must name the threshold, the action, the owner, and the time bound. Then state, in one sentence, why the hotel's current "email the facilities manager" approach fails the same objective.
