# U2 L25: Unit 2 Review Day 2 (Nov 10, Tue — PAPER DAY)

**⚡ HW Quiz (8 min):** Graded HW quiz — 5 MCQ on 2.3 protecting physical spaces and 2.4 detecting physical attacks, plus 2 short FRQ items on treatment selection.

**Learning Objectives:**
- 2.3.B Evaluate surveillance coverage by placement and viewing angle, not by camera count
- 2.3.C Apply environmental and monitoring controls, including Taiwan typhoon and flood season conditions
- 2.4.C Set an alerting threshold and describe the response it triggers

**Materials:** Graded HW quiz (printed), physical security FRQ prompt, FRQ scoring rubric, one model answer, study guide

**No computers today.** Paper day.

---

## Activities

1. **Graded HW quiz (8 min):** As written on the board. Collected.

2. **FRQ — deconstruct the prompt, then write (17 min):**
   - **Prompt:** A 40-unit apartment building in Taichung has one entry door with a keypad, a CCTV camera at the parking entrance, and a badge log for staff. Residents report that unknown people have entered several times in the past month. The building's switch closet is on the ground floor. Write a risk assessment for the building and recommend treatment for two of the findings you identify.
   - **Deconstruct (3 min), whole class, on the board:** what is being asked, and what is *not*. It asks for a risk assessment — likelihood and impact, not a description. It asks for exactly two treatments, not five. It does not ask you to redesign the building. Write the three requirements at the top of the page; every sentence after that should serve one of them.
   - **Write (10 min):** Ten minutes, timed, on paper. Silent. If you have time left over, improve a justification rather than adding a third finding.
   - **Peer-score (4 min):** Swap with a partner and score against the rubric on the back of your page. Score the *partner's* paper honestly — the rubric is the arbiter, not your opinion of their building.

3. **Model answer (10 min):** Read the model answer aloud. Then the important part: have students score their own paper against the rubric a second time and note one specific difference between their answer and the model.
   - **Rubric (10 points total):**
     - Identifies at least two physical vulnerabilities correctly and labels the CED category (2.2.A, 2.2.B, 2.2.C) — 2 pts
     - Gives a likelihood and an impact for each, with a one-sentence reason — 2 pts
     - Selects a correct treatment (avoid / transfer / mitigate / accept) for each, with justification — 4 pts
     - Addresses the ground-floor switch closet and a typhoon/flood or shared-building-network condition — 2 pts
   - The rubric is worth as much as the answer. The two points for the Taiwan conditions are the most commonly lost: the model answer treats the ground-floor closet as a flood and drainage problem, not only a security problem.

4. **Targeted re-teach (10 min):** Re-teach the **two weakest learning objectives from today's quiz only**. Read the item analysis first, pick the two lowest percentages, and spend the whole block there. Do not re-teach what they already have. Each re-teach: the missed misconception stated in one sentence, then a fresh example, then a 30-second check question on paper.

   - Reserve **2.4.C alerting thresholds** as one of the two slots if it underperforms — it is the objective the class most often treats as "set it and forget it." The real content is what happens after the alert fires: who is paged, how fast, and what gets logged as evidence.

**Homework (due Wed 20:30 — Wednesday quiz):** Write a six-question practice MCQ set covering 2.1 and 2.2, with rationales. These are your own — bring them Wednesday and use them to peer-grade. Finish the study guide if incomplete.

**Differentiation / ELL support:** The deconstruct step is what makes the FRQ tractable, so give every student the three-requirement scaffold on the page. The rubric is printed on the reverse so peer-scoring is mechanical rather than judgmental. The re-teach is driven by live item analysis, so the objectives it covers are announced at the start of the block and students know where to aim their attention. Students who finish early should write a *third* finding on the ground-floor closet, not another treatment.
