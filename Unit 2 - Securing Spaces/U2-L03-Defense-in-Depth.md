# U2 L3: Defense in Depth (Oct 2, Fri — TECH DAY)

**⚡ HW Quiz (5 min):** 3 MCQ on risk assessment (likelihood vs impact, inherent vs residual, qualitative vs quantitative).

**Learning Objectives:**
- 2.B Describe defense in depth and classify controls by layer and by function

**Materials:** Slides, diagram templates ([GDoc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/18Iiy63w55sCT4jyE4ulqMQ6D3pzKmjJb0pvc8ekH_hk/edit)), whiteboard, your risk matrix from Thursday

---

## Activities

1. **Hook (5 min):** "One lock on your door, or three?" Get a show of hands on which is better, then ask what the *right* answer depends on. The activity is the answer.

2. **Direct instruction (15 min):**
   - **Layers** — physical, technical, managerial. Name examples at each: locks and badge readers; passwords, MFA, firewalls; policies, training, background checks.
   - **Functions** — preventative (stops it happening), detective (finds it happening), corrective (responds after). A single control can serve more than one function, and the distinction is not always clean.
   - **The castle analogy:** walls, gate, guards, moat. The wall is not the defense — the *combination* is. No single layer holds forever, so the design assumes each one eventually fails.
   - Connect back to Thursday: a control **reduces residual risk**. Inherent risk is before; residual is after. Ask students to name which layer their top-three Thursday scenarios are actually reduced by.

3. **Activity — layered defense mapping (20 min):** Scenario: protect the school's grade database. In groups, students use the diagram template to build a layered defense, then label every control they placed as preventative, detective, or corrective.

   Require at least one control per layer and at least one detective control — most groups default to prevention only, which is exactly the weakness defense-in-depth is meant to fix. Push on the gaps: *if your preventive layer fails completely, what detects it?*

4. **Exit ticket (5 min):** "Why is defense-in-depth more effective than a single strong control?" Collect on paper.

**Homework (due Sun 20:30 — Monday quiz):** Finish the defense-in-depth diagram if incomplete. Read CED 2.3–2.4 in preparation for Monday's flex day.

**Differentiation / ELL support:** The diagram template is fillable, so students can place controls as text labels rather than drawing. The layer/function distinction is the hard part — a printed reference table of the three functions with a plain-language example each is available. Pair students so a confident writer records while the group decides placement.
