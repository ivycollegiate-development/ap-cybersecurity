## Activities

1. **Ground rules (3 min):** Closed notes. One pass — do not go back and change an answer unless you are told to. Five minutes. You are being scored on retrieval, not on keeping your study guide open.

2. **Sprint (5 min):** Five timed MCQs across Unit 2, one per section, weighted toward 2.4 detection and 2.1 risk, which is where the marks are. Timer on the board; call time when it goes.

3. **Peer-grade against the key (10 min):** Swap papers. Grade with the answer key. The key gives the CED rationale for every option, including the wrong ones — read the rationale, do not just mark the letter.

4. **Rationale re-read (20 min):** For every item you missed, re-read the CED rationale and find the sentence in the rationale that explains the error. Then file the miss in your Unit 2 study guide: question number, the option you chose, the correct answer, and the objective code. This is the whole assignment and it takes ten minutes. The remaining time is for the ones you are still shaky on.

5. **Re-teach board and close (7 min):** I record the objective codes of the most-missed items on the re-teach board. **Those codes are the Nov 9–12 review block.** If 2.4.B is on that board, four days of review will be built around it. Treat the board as the study plan, not as a score.

**Homework (due Wed 20:30 — Oct 28 quiz):** Finish filing today's misses in your Unit 2 study guide. Every miss, not just the graded ones — if you got it right by guessing, file it too. Tomorrow's synthesis day assumes you can look up any objective in the guide.

**Differentiation / ELL support:** The answer key rationales are written as full sentences so they can be read aloud or re-read for comprehension. Peer grading can be done in pairs with the rationale read aloud for students who prefer audio. Students who miss all five: file all five and see me after class — that pattern is useful information, not a failure.
