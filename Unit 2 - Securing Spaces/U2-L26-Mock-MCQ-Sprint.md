# U2 L26: Mock MCQ Sprint (Nov 11, Wed — PAPER DAY)

**⚡ HW Quiz (5 min):** Peer-grading setup — collect the 6-question practice sets students wrote for homework. You will be using them to grade each other in about 25 minutes, so bring them.

**Learning Objectives:**
- 2.1.F Apply the four risk treatment options correctly to a described scenario
- 2.2.C Evaluate the physical attacks that bypass access controls without touching them
- 2.4.B Correlate video and log evidence to reconstruct what happened during an access event

**Materials:** Mock MCQ sprint (5 questions, printed, one per student), the Oct 27 sprint sheet (returned), rationales, red/yellow/green tracking sheet, student-written practice sets

**No computers today.** Paper day. **Timed.** Five questions, six minutes, no notes, no phone.

---

## Activities

1. **Sprint instructions (3 min):** Five questions, six minutes total. Each question maps to one Unit 2 objective. Answer all five — no penalty for a guess, penalty for a blank. I am timing this, not grading it for speed beyond the six minutes. You get the same six minutes you had on Oct 27 so the comparison is fair.

2. **Mock MCQ sprint (6 min):** Silent, timed, on paper.

3. **Peer-grade (7 min):** Swap papers with the student next to you. Grade five, read the rationale, and if you think the key is wrong, write your objection in the margin. I will read the objections. An objection that is right is worth more than an objecting student.

4. **Rationale re-read (6 min):** Re-read every rationale, including the ones you got right. This is deliberate: the sprint is not a score, it is a diagnostic. Read the rationale for the question you got right too, because a correct answer from weak reasoning will cost you on Friday.

5. **Immediate re-teach of the most-missed objective (13 min):** Take the single objective with the lowest class percentage and re-teach it, in this block, while it is fresh. State the misconception in one sentence, work one fresh example end to end, then run a 3-question check on paper with a short answer key.

   The delta conversation follows: **each student writes down which objective moved from red to yellow since Oct 27.** Use the tracking sheet with the three columns. Name the objective in CED terms (2.1.F, 2.2.C, 2.4.B), not "the treatment one."

6. **Delta read-out and close (10 min):** Two or three students read their red-to-yellow objective out loud. Then the honest part: **the reverse** — anyone who moved the wrong way, or who has been red on the same objective twice, names it privately to me before Friday. Being red on one objective on Nov 11 is a solvable problem. Being red on it and not saying so is not.

**Homework (due Thu 20:30 — Thursday quiz):** Re-do the question you missed on today's sprint, from memory, and write two sentences explaining what the rationale was telling you. Then bring your study guide to its final form — complete, in your own words, covering 2.1 through 2.4.

**Differentiation / ELL support:** Peer-grading is structured by a printed answer key with space for objections, so a student who does not yet trust their own judgment has an anchor. The re-teach is chosen from the live item analysis, so the objective it covers is announced at the start of the block rather than being a surprise. The red/yellow/green sheet stays with the student across the week so progress is visible to them, not only to the teacher. Students who finish the six-minute sprint early sit with the rationale re-read, not idle.
