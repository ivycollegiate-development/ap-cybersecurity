# U2 L16: Mini-Project Day 2 — Peer Review and Revision (Oct 21, Wed — TECH DAY)

**⚡ HW Quiz (5 min):** 3 MCQ on physical security policy and environmental controls, from the CED 2.3 material.

**Learning Objectives:**
- 2.3.A Evaluate a peer group's layered physical security plan against stated criteria and score it
- 2.3.C Judge whether a plan's environmental and monitoring controls are proportionate to the site
- 2.4.C Critique another plan's alerting thresholds and residual risk treatment
- Apply structured review feedback to revise a design under a fixed budget

**Materials:** Slides, peer review rubric (printed, one per student), review form, scenario brief, Google Slides decks, projector, timer on the board

---

## Activities

1. **Frame the review (5 min):** Two points before anything moves.

   First: **you are reviewing a plan, not a person.** The plan is a document with a fixed budget and a customer audit attached, and the question is whether it holds up under someone else's reading. Say the useful thing plainly — "the detection section has thresholds but nobody receives the 3 AM alert, so the detection is theater" is a gift to that group.

   Second: **the review is a deliverable, not a favour.** Every group submits a completed rubric. Vague praise is not feedback, and a rubric with no written comments is a wasted period.

2. **Finish the draft (8 min):** Groups complete the deck in place. Anyone with a thin server room section or an unfinished cost table finishes that first. Teacher circulates and checks each group against the two flags from yesterday. Quick check-ins, one sentence each: *what is missing, and who is finishing it.*

3. **Swap and review (15 min):** Deck swaps to another group. Reviewers work alone and silently for 10 minutes on the printed rubric, then 5 minutes in pairs to compare scores and argue to agreement. Reviewers score the deck they received; they do not talk to the authors yet.

   **Peer Review Rubric — 40 points**

   | # | Criterion | Points | What full credit looks like |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | **Layer coverage** | 6 | All six sections present and substantive: perimeter, entry, interior, server room, policies, detection. No section is a placeholder. |
   | 2 | **Defense in depth is real** | 6 | Each layer has at least one control, and the plan states what detects each control's failure. Not prevention only. |
   | 3 | **Server room treatment** | 6 | Access scoping, door contact and tamper detection, environmental/flood risk, power and HVAC failure response. |
   | 4 | **Cost realism** | 6 | Cost table complete, unit prices plausible for Taiwan, **total under NT$2,000,000**, contingency 3–8%. |
   | 5 | **Value for money** | 4 | Every line names what it defends. The camera and reader count is justified by purpose, not by "more is safer." |
   | 6 | **Detection quality** | 5 | Alerting thresholds reference real patterns (shared badge, tailgating, failed-then-success, after-hours), **and the out-of-hours recipient is named.** |
   | 7 | **Policies and auditability** | 3 | Policies have owners and a review cycle. The plan can produce records an auditor accepts. |
   | 8 | **Shared-surface realism** | 2 | The bike area, courier bay, and off-site cabinet are addressed as constraints the team does not fully control. |
   | 9 | **Clarity of presentation** | 2 | Deck is legible, sections are findable, someone outside the group could follow the argument. |
   | | **Total** | **40** | |

   **Reviewer requirement:** every criterion scored below 60% of its points must carry one sentence of *specific* written feedback naming the section and the fix. "Section 2 is weak" is not acceptable; "no visitor escort procedure in Section 2, and the audit will ask for one" is.

   Note criterion 6 — the out-of-hours recipient. It is deliberately the easiest marker of whether a group actually thought about detection or just listed thresholds, and it separates the decks that are strong from the decks that are complete.

4. **Feedback exchange and iteration (13 min):** Decks return to authors with the rubric attached. Authors read the feedback, then have 8 minutes to revise.

   Rules for the revision round, and enforce them:
   - **Fix the highest-value feedback, not all of it.** A group cannot absorb 40 points of critique in 8 minutes. Each group picks the two or three rubric lines where the points lost are cheapest to recover, and does those.
   - **A disagreement with the reviewer is allowed** — but it must be reasoned. If the authors keep a design, they note why in one line on the slide. That is a stronger artifact than a deck revised into mediocrity by someone else's opinion.
   - **The cost table is closed.** If a revision adds spend, something else comes out. NT$2M is still the ceiling and it is still the real constraint.

5. **Report-out and close (4 min):** Each group reports three numbers: their original score, their revised score, and the single change that moved it. Then the teacher names the two most common weaknesses across all decks, which is the transferable lesson.

   Tomorrow, Day 3, is presentations — so the expected state going out of this period is a **finished, revised deck with a cost table under budget and the reviewers' key comments visible.** A group that has not finished revising is not ready to present.

**Homework (due Thu 20:30 — Thursday quiz):** Finalize the deck for presentation tomorrow: 8–10 slides, one presenter per section, the cost table intact and under NT$2M, and a 30-second closing statement on what your plan protects and what it accepts as residual risk. Bring any open questions on residual risk — that is what the Q&A will probe.

**Differentiation / ELL support:** The rubric is printed with full-credit descriptors, so the review decision is anchored to stated criteria rather than to taste — this is the main scaffold and it makes the review defensible. The review is silent and individual before the pair discussion, which gives quieter students a written route into the conversation. Students who need support can review with a partner, taking one half of the rubric sections, and then merge scores. The revision round is short by design: students who were overwhelmed by feedback get to select two items rather than triage forty, and the "disagreement is allowed, but reason it" rule protects students who think their original design was fine. Presenters can be assigned during the report-out so no student is forced to present a section they did not own.
