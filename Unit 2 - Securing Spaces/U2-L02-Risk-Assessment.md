## Activities

1. **Hook (5 min):** Play **PBS NewsHour — "What we know about the cyberattacks on water systems in 7 states"** (https://youtu.be/4cqSk0EGH10, 6:33, Aug 3 2026; free, no paywall): FBI confirms at least 7 states targeted; Iran the likely culprit; former FBI cyber official Cynthia Kaiser on the scale and attribution.

   Then the framing: **"In Aug 2026, state-linked hackers hit U.S. water systems in at least 7 states. Nobody died and the water stayed safe — but utilities ran manual operations for days. How do you put a number on a risk that could, in a worse case, contaminate a city's water? That's what risk assessment is for."**

   *Teacher background: NYT, Aug 1 2026 — print/PDF only. Students get the video instead of the paywalled article.*

2. **Direct instruction (15 min):**
   - **Risk = Likelihood × Impact.** Likelihood: how probable is this in a given period? Impact: how bad is it if it happens?
   - **Qualitative** (High/Medium/Low) — fast, uses judgment, comparable across a team.
   - **Quantitative** ($$, downtime hours, records exposed) — harder to produce, harder to argue with.
   - **Documentation formats:** risk register, risk matrix (likelihood × impact grid), risk heat map.
   - Note the two axes are independent. A rare-but-catastrophic event is low likelihood, high impact — and the matrix exists precisely to keep those from being averaged away.

3. **Activity — risk matrix exercise (20 min):** Six threat scenarios for a school: ransomware, stolen device, phishing, power outage, physical break-in, insider threat. Students rate likelihood and impact, plot each on the matrix, and prioritize the top three.

   **Extension:** add the Aug 2026 water-system hack as a seventh scenario — internet-exposed SCADA/PLC controllers at a small utility. Discussion prompt: the likelihood of a foreign-power attack on a small town seemed low a year ago. How does a real incident change the likelihood rating for every other utility?

   This also covers the CIA triad in a concrete setting: **availability** impact (manual operations, boil-water notices) and **integrity** impact (chemical treatment levels) — note that integrity failure is the quiet one, because altered treatment levels may not be visible in the water.

4. **Exit ticket (5 min):** "What's the difference between inherent risk and residual risk?" Inherent = before controls. Residual = after controls. Collect these — the wording recurs on Friday and on the Unit 2 test.

**Homework (due Wed 20:30 — Friday quiz):** Keep your prioritized risk matrix. Friday's mapping activity will reuse it.

**Differentiation / ELL support:** Provide a partially completed matrix with likelihood pre-rated and only impact blank, for students who need the task narrowed. The residual-risk question works well orally for ELL students before written response.
